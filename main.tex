\section{Theory}
%% =================================================

The procedure described below is motivated by the reference energy correction scheme in TC theory [cite johannes], that states that the basis set error in correlation energy converges quickly with TC, while the errors in the reference energy (i.e., the Hartree-Fock energy) is untouched.  To avoid calculations in multiple basis sets and make the workflow more simple and practical, we solve the reference state in a large basis set, and then construct a virtual space with size equal to a lower-level basis. This keeps the reference accurate, while allowing us to perform the correlation calculation in a smaller virtual space that is more similar to the small basis set. 

The selected virtual space is constructed by projecting the large-basis virtual
MOs onto the small-basis AO space via
%
\begin{equation}
  M = S_{11}^{-1/2} S_{12} C^{\text{virt}}
  \qquad [n_{\text{small}} \times n_{\text{large,virt}}]
\end{equation}
%
where $S_{11}$ and $S_{12}$ are the small-basis and cross-overlap matrices,
and $C^{\text{virt}}$ collects the large-basis virtual MO coefficients.
The elements $M_{\mu a} = \langle\tilde\varphi_\mu|\psi_a\rangle$ are overlaps
between L\"{o}wdin-orthonormalized small AOs and $S_{22}$-orthonormal
large-basis virtuals, so the thin SVD
%
\begin{equation}
  M = U\Sigma V^{\top}
  \qquad
  U\ [n_{\text{small}}\times n_{\text{small}}],\;
  \Sigma\ [n_{\text{small}}\times n_{\text{small}}],\;
  V\ [n_{\text{large,virt}}\times n_{\text{small}}]
\end{equation}
%
yields singular values $\sigma_k\leq 1$ that are exact cosines of the principal
angles between the two subspaces. Dropping the $n_{\text{occ}}$ columns of $V$
with near-zero $\sigma_k$ and projecting onto the AO basis gives the
$n_{\text{sel}}=n_{\text{small}}-n_{\text{occ}}$ selected virtuals
%
\begin{equation}
  C^{\text{sel}} = C^{\text{virt}} V_{\text{kept}}
  \qquad [n_{\text{large,AO}}\times n_{\text{sel}}]
\end{equation}
%
which are $S_{22}$-orthonormal by construction. The SVD rotation mixes
canonical virtuals, rendering the projected Fock matrix
%
\begin{equation}
  F^{\text{sel}} = V_{\text{kept}}^{\top}\,\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_a)\,V_{\text{kept}}
  \qquad [n_{\text{sel}}\times n_{\text{sel}}]
\end{equation}
%
non-diagonal. Diagonalizing $F^{\text{sel}} = W\tilde\Lambda W^{\top}$ yields
the semicanonical orbitals
%
\begin{equation}
  C^{\text{final}} = C^{\text{virt}}\,V_{\text{kept}}\,W
  \qquad [n_{\text{large,AO}}\times n_{\text{sel}}].
\end{equation}

\section{Atomization energies}
%% ============================================================

We compute atomization energies with several xTC method families and benchmark
them against SHCI+PBE+CV reference values. All calculations employ
effective core potentials (ECPs) for the third-row elements.
The SHCI, SHCI+PBE, and conventional CCSD(T) results used here as references
are taken from Ref.~\cite{Yao2020}; the SHCI+PBE energies extrapolated to the
complete basis set (CBS) limit are considered the best available benchmark
for the G2/55 set.
Per-basis, per-molecule method discrepancies and the full basis set convergence
of error metrics are hosted as interactive plotly figures~\cite{interactivefigs}.
